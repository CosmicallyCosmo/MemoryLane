What it does

Three moments, all in your family's own voice.

No app to open, nothing to remember. The trigger is what you're already doing: looking at something, or asking out loud.

Memories live in objects

A relative photographs a keepsake and records a message. Ask “What's this?” and it plays. If the glasses notice it on their own, they offer it first.

“This was Mum's teapot. Every Sunday after your walk…”

Asked once, answered from then on

A question nobody has answered goes to the family, with how often it's been asked. Someone answers once, and the glasses give that answer every time after, in their voice.

“Margaret's at the hairdresser's, Dad. She'll be home for lunch.”

“Who is that?”

Ask “Who's that?” about a framed photo and hear that person's own recorded hello. Each relative can add a memory lane of clips from their life to walk through.

“Hi Dad, it's Sarah, your daughter.”

Why glasses

The phone is the step that fails.

To use a phone app, someone with memory loss would first have to remember the phone. On glasses, there's nothing to find or open: the trigger is what they're already doing.

  • Bare text, two choices at mostThe lens never shows more than a title and two buttons.
  • Everything written is spokenAnd everything spoken is written. The whole loop works on glasses with no display.
  • Calm by designA memory is offered, never sprung. Taking the glasses off pauses everything.

For the family

See how the day went, from anywhere.

The family app shows the questions waiting, the answers given, the memories opened most, and the things Dad keeps looking at that hold no memory yet.

What we promise

The AI chooses. The family speaks.

The model picks which object is in view and which recorded answer fits. It never writes a word the wearer hears, and no voice is synthesised.

Every word is theirs

Recordings play as recorded. A typed answer is read by a neutral voice that says who wrote it.

Honest when unsure

If nothing fits, it says so with a fixed line. A wrong answer is worse than none.

No face recognition

Telling someone a stranger is their daughter would do real harm. Only a framed photo says who's who.

Agreement first

No frame is looked at until the wearer, or whoever holds their legal authority, has agreed.

Works offline

The phone keeps memories, hellos and answers, so they still play with no connection.

Everything can be deleted

Frames are never kept. Removing a memory removes its uploads from Cloudinary too.

Where it stands

What we've checked, and what we haven't.

Memory Lane is a support and comfort aid, not a treatment. It has not been tried with people living with dementia, and we claim no benefit.

Checked

  • The whole loop on an Android emulator with simulated glasses and live Gemini
  • Recognition, the offer and replay on a physical phone with simulated glasses
  • Memory lanes planned by live Gemini and cut by Cloudinary
  • 95 server tests and a 21-check end-to-end test

Not yet

  • Real glasses everything ran on Meta's Mock Device Kit
  • Real people never tried with someone living with dementia
  • Recognition in a real home only test photos have been scored
  • Heart rate only from simulated motion, labelled as such
  • A real family the demo family is made up: voices by ElevenLabs, some films by Kling AI
Under the hood

Glasses, a phone, a server, and two APIs doing exactly one kind of job.

The glasses app is Kotlin on Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit. A Node server keeps the household. Gemini makes choices; Cloudinary makes films. Scroll on, or go deep on the technology page.

1

Which object is this?

Family's reference photos plus one frame from the glasses. Returns a memory, or “none”.

2

Which answer fits?

The question and the family's recorded answers. Returns an answer id, or “none”.

3

Arrange a memory lane

It hears the voice message and places each photo where its subject is mentioned.

4

Write down a message

A word-for-word draft, which a relative reads and saves before it's used.

The rule, in code

A schema, at temperature 0.

Generative calls answer JSON against a response schema at temperature 0; recognition is a closed choice on the Decisions API. Either way the model returns a choice or a plan, never words for the wearer. Below a confidence of 0.6, a match counts as no match.
