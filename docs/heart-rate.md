# Heart rate from head motion

How Memory Lane reads a pulse from the glasses, and what has and has not been checked.

The glasses have no pulse sensor and the Device Access Toolkit has no heart-rate call. It does have **Motion** (`mwdat-motion`, experimental): the glasses' accelerometer and gyroscope at up to 60 readings a second. Each heartbeat makes the head recoil very slightly, and a still head shows it. This is ballistocardiography, and it was shown on Google Glass in 2014 (Hernandez, Li, Rehg and Picard, "BioGlass", about one beat a minute of error on 12 people sitting, lying and standing still). Memory Lane reads the pulse that way.

- `PulseEstimator` (`android/.../MotionPulse.kt`) keeps the short ringing each beat leaves (10 to 13 Hz) on all three axes of each sensor, and finds how often its strength repeats over the last ten seconds. It answers once a second, or not at all: nothing while the head is turning, nothing when no steady beat stands out.
- `GlassesViewModel.pulseFromMotion` starts Motion on the session at 60 Hz and feeds it. `PulseReader` measures from the samples' own timestamps how fast they really arrive and works to that rate; below 28 a second it gives no reading. Samples from a Neural Band are left out: a wrist is not a head. On real glasses that is the only source. If Motion will not start, heart rate is simply not read; nothing is made up.
- **Simulated glasses** have no head, so Mock Device Kit replays made-up head motion with a heartbeat in it (`setMotionFeed`), with a rise while a memory is open three times in four. The pulse is read from that by the same code, and every session is marked `simulated`, which the family app shows beside the chart. If the replay gives nothing within 30 seconds the app says so in its log and falls back to made-up numbers, also marked `simulated`.
- **What has been checked:** the estimator against made-up motion, in `MotionPulseTest` (`./gradlew testDebugUnitTest`): steady rates from 52 to 126, a beat a third as strong, a rise and fall, samples arriving at half the rate asked for, no reading from noise, no reading while moving. **What has not:** any real glasses or any person. Nobody has reported doing this on Meta glasses, the signal there may be weaker than on Glass, and Motion must be enabled for the app in the Wearables Developer Center and needs recent glasses firmware. On real glasses the first thing to do is sit still for a minute beside a pulse oximeter and compare.
- It is read only while the glasses are connected and **Note heart rate** is on under Privacy. Motion samples are used and dropped as they arrive. The last minute of readings is held in memory on the phone to give a resting rate, and nothing else is kept from outside a memory.
- Readings stop whenever the wearer moves. A gap of up to eight seconds is bridged with the reading before it; a session with a longer gap is not sent.
- A session is the resting rate from just before a memory opened and a reading every two seconds while it was open. It is sent as a `heart_response` event with the other events.
- The server (`server/src/heart.js`) turns a session into numbers: resting, peak, average, when the peak came, and whether it rose (8 or more beats a minute above resting, held for about ten seconds), fell (average 5 or more below) or stayed steady.
- The family sees it on each memory's page, as "Heart rate rose" on a memory's tile, in a list on Home of the memories it rose during at least twice, and as at most one notification per memory per day.
- The words state what was measured and never name a feeling. Moving or talking raises heart rate too, and the page says so.
